# Writing

The reader knows nothing and may open any card first. Write so that each sentence makes sense in one read (the 7-year-old test for hints).

## Words
- **Explain every word where it's used.** Every card explains its own words (volt, kB, mA, LED…), even if another card already did. Explain with an everyday picture, then give the real term: "how hard electricity pushes (volts)".
- **Everyday words count too.** Don't assume a novice holds "wall power", "mains", "socket" or a cable type: one asked "what does power from the wall even mean?" Say "the electricity from an ordinary wall socket".
- **One word for one thing, everywhere.** Choose the names before writing, and keep to them in every chapter. One course called the same thing four different names before anyone noticed. Search for it in the check.
- **Demo labels are writing too.** The words inside a demo follow every rule here: concrete, no jargon.
- **Plain explanation before a technical term** (I2C, QSPI, PWM): the term may follow in brackets or small grey text.
- **Numbers come from the fact sheet** and match it exactly; an unsure number says where it comes from. Units in words the reader knows (3.3 volts, 2 MB is room for about 2 million letters).

## The real thing first
Name the real part and say where it is before any comparison. The comparison helps understanding; it never replaces the name or becomes the title.

## General or this device only
Whenever a number or behaviour could be mistaken as universal, say whether it is. "Every board needs steady power, but not always 3.3 volts: the Arduino Uno works at 5 volts."

## Every example has a purpose
Each demo, story and project step proves one concrete fact and says what job the device does in it. Give a real use ("a robot's headlight", "a bumper switch").

## Say only what helps
- **One big idea per picture**, in one line under it. If it needs two lines, it needs two pictures.
- **No how-to-use or behind-the-scenes text** ("This page lets you…", "We built this with…").
- **Don't describe what the learner can already see** ("Only the Connect parts are showing"). The picture already says it.
- **Unknown or approximate:** an unknown fact goes in the card's small note line; an approximate shape gets one quiet note under the 3D view; simulated numbers say so once (in the project's first step and the part's card).

## People
- The page speaks to the learner, kindly and plainly.
- **The learner's own record may personalise their course** (compare with a device they own). Nothing from one learner's record goes into another learner's course or into this skill.

## Languages
- **Write each language natively, from the meaning.** Don't write one language and translate it sentence by sentence: translated text keeps the other language's sentence shapes and reads stiffly. Short sentences, everyday words, natural names for parts.
- Chinese: follow `zh-style.md`.
- After writing, do a pass that reads **only one language at a time**, looking for stiff sentences, sentences starting with "it", stacked colons, formal words, and comparisons or examples that need background the reader doesn't have ("how far is the wall?" made the learner ask "which wall?").
- When the learner is a native reader, ask at each review stop which sentences read badly. For a language nobody involved reads, say so at hand-over ("This Japanese was written by AI and hasn't been checked by a native reader").
